We presented the first GPU port of LESGO, a pseudo-spectral wind-farm
large-eddy simulation solver comprising nearly 70{,}000 lines of
Fortran code. We demonstrated that porting such a legacy codebase 
requires a fine-grained data-management strategy. Therefore, we 
identified four data-management operations, 
and classified every host--device coupling point in LESGO
into these solutions. In addition, we developed targeted kernel
optimizations including a chunk-pipelined tridiagonal solver, a
batched actuator-line model, batched-FFT dynamic subgrid-scale
filtering, and CPU--GPU heterogeneous overlap, raising overall GPU
utilization to 93\%. We believe these optimization principles extend
beyond LESGO to broader multiphysics solvers. On a production-scale 
benchmark, our GPU implementation delivers up to
32$\times$ speedup over the fastest CPU baseline. Finally, we
presented realistic physical simulation results to demonstrate the
practical value and scientific impact of our porting effort for
atmospheric flow research.
